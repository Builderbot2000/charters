# Discussion venues for the Social Charter

This record lists the places where serious people could discuss the Social
Charter, and which part of the project each place suits. The author has so
far developed the project alone, with AI as the only outside input, and wants
human review from people who know the problem and the literature.

Status as of October 28, 2026. The venue details come from web searches on
that date. Check each venue's current schedule before you rely on it.

## Selection criteria

A venue qualifies if its members know at least one of the project's source
literatures, such as polycentric governance, constitutional political
economy, mechanism design, or AI governance. Mainstream political forums,
such as Reddit, are excluded. Discussion there rarely engages with the
problem or the literature.

## Notes on the network state community

The network state community uses the project's vocabulary of charters,
jurisdictions, and exit, and some of its members have written charters in
practice. Two parts of the framework make it a useful test audience:

- Balaji Srinivasan's path, from a startup society to a network state that
  seeks diplomatic recognition, follows the voluntary admission end of the
  formation gradient.
- Its founder-led model is a live case of the decision that democracy is a
  design choice. See [undemocratic-higher-charter.md](undemocratic-higher-charter.md).

The community has strong priors toward exit and markets. Bring it the parts it
will challenge, such as arbitration and power balancing, not only the parts
it will agree with.

## Approach

These venues respond better to a short, specific paper than to a whole
framework. The suggested approach has three steps:

1. Write a working paper of 10 to 15 pages that frames the Social Charter as
   a prescriptive extension of IAD, built around one testable claim, such as
   the formation gradient or the enemy test.
1. Post the paper on SSRN or PhilPapers.
1. Send the paper to the authors in the preceding table, with one specific
   question each, and use their replies to find seminars that will host a
   presentation.
